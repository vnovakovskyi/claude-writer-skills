# AI tells — universal

Tells that work in any language. Read this file together with the file for the
text's language in this folder (`en.md`, `uk.md`, `ru.md`), and only that one.

A single instance is rarely a problem; the tell is **density and repetition**.
Judge every item by the norms of the text's language: what is a tell in English
can be plain grammar in Ukrainian or Russian, and the language file says so.

## Typography and formatting

- **Dashes as a dramatic pause or aside** in sentence after sentence — beyond
  what the language's grammar needs (the language file says where that line is).
- **Bold inside running text** several times per paragraph; **every bullet opens
  with a bold label and a period** ("**Performance.** …").
- **Emoji** as bullets or in headings. An emoji the author clearly places on
  purpose (a recurring label or marker) is not a tell.
- **Headings that are pithy sentences**, and **every section ending on a
  one-line takeaway**.
- **Fancy Unicode in prose:** arrows (→), "≪", the single-character ellipsis (…),
  mixed curly and straight quotes.
- **Lists where prose would do**, nested lists, every list exactly three to five
  items.
- **Colon before a reveal:** "The rule is simple:", "Here's the thing:".

## Constructions

- **Negation–correction antithesis:** "It's not X — it's Y", "X is not A. It is
  B.", "not just X, but Y". The strongest single tell once it repeats.
- **Rule of three:** triads of adjectives, nouns, or clauses everywhere.
- **Rhetorical question answered at once:** "Why? Because…".
- **Symmetrical paired sentences:** "X gives you A without B. Y gives you C without D."
- **Aphoristic closers, the moral of the story:** "And that's the whole point.",
  "In the end, it all comes down to trust."
- **Signposting and meta-talk:** "Let's break it down", "Let's dive in", "In this
  article we will…", "Here is what X looks like:", "A few points worth
  mentioning:".
- **Restating what was just said:** "In other words…", "So, …" followed by the
  same idea again.
- **Over-smooth transitions** gluing every paragraph to the next.
- **Hedge words and weight words:** "it's worth noting", "importantly",
  "crucially", "essentially".

## Rhythm

- **Uniform sentences:** almost all 12–25 words, no fragments, no long run-ons.
- **Uniform paragraphs:** the same length and the same shape (claim → explanation
  → takeaway).
- **Fake staccato:** a string of one-line paragraphs for drama.

## Absence of a person

- **No specifics:** no names, dates, versions, or numbers from the author's own
  work, no "I got this wrong", no story of how they found out.
- **Generic "you"** everywhere; the first person appears only in the intro and
  the outro.
- **One flat level of confidence:** no opinion, no doubt, no humour, no irony.
- **Cheerleading:** "game-changer", "powerful", exclamation marks.

## Register

- **Nominalisations and abstract nouns** instead of verbs ("the implementation
  of the verification process").
- **Passive voice** where the actor is known.
- **Bureaucratic or academic vocabulary** in a text meant to be conversational.
